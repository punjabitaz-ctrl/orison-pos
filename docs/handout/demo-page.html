The hard part

What happens when the line drops mid-sale

Most systems stop. This one doesn't, and it doesn't lose or double-count anything when the connection returns.

01

Sale is rung

Written to the terminal and queued. The cashier carries on with the next customer.

02

Line returns

The queue sends itself. Nobody presses anything, and stock levels catch up.

03

Two tills disagree

Same phone sold twice? First one through is kept. The second is voided, its stock restored.

04

Manager is told

The clash is logged for review with the device and time. Nothing is silently dropped.

Everything else it does

The rest of the shop, covered

IMEI tracking

Phones and laptops are tracked one unit at a time. Selling one captures its serial and binds it to that sale for life — through history, refunds and receipts.

Customer accounts

Net-30 terms, a running ledger, collections, and 30/60/90+ day ageing so you know who owes what and for how long.

Customer display

A second screen faces the shopper and mirrors the cart live. Cost and margin never cross to it.

Time clock

Staff punch in and out from any terminal. Nobody can punch for anyone else, and hours feed the per-cashier performance view.

Stock take & ageing

Count the shelf and post the variance, valued at cost. See what has been sitting 90+ days before it becomes a write-off.

Shelf labels

Code 128 barcodes with name and price, printed as a sheet straight from the products screen.

Runs on what the shop already owns

Install it from the browser on any phone, tablet or computer and it behaves like an app — full screen, its own icon, working offline. The back end lives in the shop's own Google account, so the data is yours and adding a terminal costs nothing.
